## Two numbers every small business should know

Big companies have finance teams. A small business or freelance practice usually has one person and a spreadsheet. These calculators cover the questions that matter most, without the spreadsheet.

**How much do I need to sell?** The <ToolLink slug="break-even-calculator">break even calculator</ToolLink> takes your fixed costs, your price and the cost of each sale, and shows how many sales you need each month to cover everything. Add a profit target and it shows the sales you need to reach that too.

**How much of my time earns money?** If you sell time, revenue depends on billable hours, not hours worked. The <ToolLink slug="utilization-rate-calculator">utilization rate calculator</ToolLink> shows what share of your available hours you bill, how far you are from a target, and what the gap is worth.

## How the two fit together

The two numbers answer each other. Break-even tells you how many projects or clients you need. Utilization tells you whether you have the hours to deliver them.

Say your break-even is 40 projects a month and each takes four hours. That's 160 billable hours. If you only have 160 available hours in a month, you'd need to bill every single one, which no business manages for long. Either the price has to rise, the costs have to fall, or you need help.

## Fixed costs, variable costs and margin

Most business finance calculators come down to three ideas.

- **Fixed costs** stay the same however much you sell: rent, software, insurance, salaries.
- **Variable costs** rise with every sale: materials, payment fees, a subcontractor per job.
- **Margin** is what each sale leaves after its variable cost, ready to pay the fixed costs.

A higher margin means fewer sales to break even. That's why a small price rise often helps more than a cost cut, and why it's worth checking your <ToolLink slug="rate-increase-calculator">rate increase</ToolLink> options too.

## Estimates, clearly explained

Each calculator shows its formula and worked examples in dollars, pounds and rupees. The examples are tested against the calculator, so the page and the result always agree. They're estimates to help you decide, not accounting advice, so check big decisions with an accountant.
