## Three numbers behind every campaign

Marketing spend is easy to track. What it buys you is harder to see. These calculators turn the numbers in your ad account and your sales records into three plain answers.

**What does a new customer cost?** The <ToolLink slug="cac-calculator">CAC calculator</ToolLink> adds up your sales and marketing spend and divides it by the new customers you won. Add what a customer is worth over time and it shows your LTV:CAC ratio, so you can see whether each customer pays back what it cost to win them.

**Do my ads pay for themselves?** The <ToolLink slug="roas-calculator">ROAS calculator</ToolLink> divides the revenue from your ads by what you spent on them. Add your profit margin and it shows your break-even ROAS: the point below which your ads lose money. It can also work backwards from a target to the revenue you need.

**What does each enquiry cost?** The <ToolLink slug="cost-per-lead-calculator">cost per lead calculator</ToolLink> divides spend by leads. Add how many leads become customers and it shows what each customer from that channel really costs.

## How the three fit together

Say you spend 2,000 on ads in a month and get 80 leads. That's 25 per lead. If one lead in ten buys, each customer costs 250. If a customer is worth 900 to you over time, you get back 3.60 for every 1 you spend to win them.

Now look at the ads alone. If those 8 customers spend 1,200 each straight away, the ads brought in 9,600 from 2,000 of spend: a ROAS of 4.8x. With a 40% margin your break-even ROAS is 2.5x, so the campaign is comfortably profitable.

## Your targets, not averages

You will find plenty of "typical" figures online. They come from other businesses with different prices, margins and customers. These calculators use only the numbers you enter, and help you set targets from your own margin and customer value.

## A few habits that help

- Use the same period for spend and results.
- Compare channels by cost per customer, not just cost per lead.
- Count every cost of winning customers, including tools and sales time.
- Re-run the numbers each month, so a rising cost shows up early.

Before you set a budget, the <ToolLink slug="break-even-calculator">break even calculator</ToolLink> shows how many sales you need each month to cover your costs.
