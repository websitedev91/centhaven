## Turning pay into numbers you can compare

Pay is quoted in different ways: an hourly wage in one advert, a yearly salary in another, a day rate for a contract. Each sounds simple on its own, but they're hard to compare until they're in the same unit and cover the same hours.

The <ToolLink slug="hourly-to-salary-calculator">hourly to salary calculator</ToolLink> converts an hourly wage into yearly, monthly, bi-weekly, weekly and daily pay, and works the other way too. A full-time year of 40 hours a week for 52 weeks is 2,080 paid hours, so $25 an hour is $52,000 a year before tax.

## Gross pay, hours and paid weeks

Three things change the answer more than people expect.

**Gross or net.** Offers are almost always quoted before tax. Compare gross with gross, and work out take-home pay separately with an official tax calculator for your country.

**Hours per week.** Many UK jobs are advertised at 37.5 hours a week, many US jobs at 40. The same salary is worth less per hour when the hours are longer, so convert salaries to an hourly rate before you compare them.

**Paid weeks.** If your holidays are paid, you're paid for all 52 weeks. Hourly workers and contractors often aren't, and two unpaid weeks cut a year's pay by almost 4%.

## Employee or contractor?

A contract day rate usually looks much higher than a salary. That's because a contractor isn't paid for holidays, public holidays, sick days or the weeks between contracts, and pays for their own pension and equipment.

The <ToolLink slug="day-rate-to-salary-calculator">day rate to salary calculator</ToolLink> takes those unpaid days out of the year and adds an allowance for benefits, so you can see what a contract is really worth next to a permanent job. If you work for yourself, the <ToolLink slug="hourly-rate-calculator">freelance hourly rate calculator</ToolLink> goes further and adds your tax and business costs.
