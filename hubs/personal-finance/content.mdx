## Start with a safety net

Most money plans go wrong at the same point: something unexpected happens and there's nothing set aside for it. A car repair, a medical bill or a month without work ends up on a credit card, and the plan stalls while the debt is paid off.

An emergency fund breaks that cycle. It's money kept for real emergencies only, usually enough to cover your essential costs for a few months. The <ToolLink slug="emergency-fund-calculator">emergency fund calculator</ToolLink> works out how big yours should be from your monthly essentials, how steady your income is and how many people depend on you, then shows how long it will take to build at your saving rate.

## Steady income or irregular income?

How much you need depends a lot on how your money arrives. A steady salary with notice periods and sick pay needs less cover than income that changes month to month.

If you're a freelancer, a contractor or paid mainly by commission, plan for longer gaps. Clients pay late, projects end suddenly and there's no employer to fall back on. A bigger buffer turns those gaps into an inconvenience rather than a crisis. ## A simple savings calculator habit

Saving works best when it's automatic and specific:

- **Know your essentials.** Rent, food, bills, insurance and loan payments: the costs you'd still have in a bad month.
- **Pick a target and a date.** A number of months of essentials, and a monthly amount you can keep up.
- **Automate it.** A transfer on payday is easier than deciding every month.
- **Keep it separate.** A separate account makes the fund harder to spend by accident.
- **Review once a year,** or when your income, costs or family change.

## Clear numbers, not advice

Every calculator here shows its formula and worked examples with real numbers in dollars, pounds and rupees, and the examples are tested against the calculator. Where a tool uses a rule of thumb, such as how many months to cover, it says so and lets you change it. The results are estimates to help you plan; for decisions about debt, investments or pensions, speak to a qualified adviser.
