## Know what you keep before you get paid

Every platform that moves your money takes a share. A freelance marketplace takes a service fee, a card processor takes a percentage and often a fixed amount per payment, and a currency conversion can add a little more. Each fee looks small on its own. Together they can take a noticeable part of what a client pays you.

These calculators show that share before it happens. Enter what your client pays, or what you want to receive, plus the fee from your own account, and each one shows the fee in money and what reaches you.

## Why you enter the fee yourself

Fees change. Platforms update their pricing, run promotions and often charge different accounts, countries or contracts differently. A number printed here could be out of date, or simply not the one that applies to you.

So we don't publish anyone's fees. You enter the percentage, and any fixed amount, shown on your proposal, offer, pricing page or statement. The calculators do the maths exactly, show every step and never guess.

## Card and payment processing fees

Card processors often charge a percentage plus a fixed fee for each payment. That fixed part matters more than it looks on small payments: on a low-value sale it can be a bigger share of the price than the percentage. A calculator for this, where you enter both parts yourself, is on the way.

## Passing fees on to clients

The simplest way to protect your income is to build fees into your price from the start. Decide what you need to take home, then use a reverse mode to find the price that gets you there:

```text
price to charge = amount you want ÷ (1 − fee %)
```

If there is a fixed fee too, add it to the amount you want before you divide. Check the platform's rules before adding a surcharge, because some don't allow it, and be clear on your quote or invoice about how the price was set.
