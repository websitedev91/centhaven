## Know what you keep before you get paid

Every platform that moves your money takes a share. A freelance marketplace takes a service fee, a card processor takes a percentage plus a fixed amount, and a currency conversion adds a little more on top. Each fee looks small on its own. Together they can take a noticeable part of what a client pays you.

These calculators show that share before it happens. Enter what your client pays, or what you want to receive, and each one shows the fee, where it comes from and what reaches you.

## Freelance marketplace fees

**Upwork** charges freelancers a service fee that it sets per contract. The <ToolLink slug="upwork-fee-calculator">Upwork fee calculator</ToolLink> uses the percentage shown on your proposal or offer, and can also work backwards to the rate you need to quote. Sending proposals costs Connects too, and the <ToolLink slug="upwork-connects-calculator">Upwork Connects calculator</ToolLink> turns them into a cost per job won.

**Fiverr** takes a share of every gig. The <ToolLink slug="fiverr-fee-calculator">Fiverr fee calculator</ToolLink> shows your earnings after that fee and the gig price you need to reach a target.

## Card payment fees

If you take card payments on your own website or invoices, the <ToolLink slug="stripe-fee-calculator">Stripe fee calculator</ToolLink> shows the fee for your country, including the extra charges for international cards and currency conversion. It can also work out what to charge so you receive an exact amount.

## Receiving money from abroad

International clients often pay through PayPal, Wise or Payoneer. Their fees depend on where you live, the currencies involved and the exchange rate used. We are adding calculators for them next, built the same way from each company's official pricing.

## How we keep fees accurate

Fees change, so every number in these calculators comes from the company's own pricing or help page. We save the link, the exact line we used and the date we checked it, and the Sources section of each calculator shows that date. Every fee page is checked again at least every three months.

The calculators use each company's standard published fees. Your own account can differ because of promotions, special pricing or how a platform sets your fee, so compare the result with what your account shows before you rely on it.

## Pricing with fees in mind

The simplest way to protect your income is to build fees into your price from the start. Decide what you need to take home, then use a reverse mode to find the price that gets you there. Pair that with the <ToolLink slug="hourly-rate-calculator">freelance hourly rate calculator</ToolLink> and your rate covers both your costs and the platform's share.
