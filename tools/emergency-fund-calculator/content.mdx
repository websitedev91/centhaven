This emergency fund calculator works out how much to keep aside for emergencies, based on your monthly essential costs, how steady your income is and how many people depend on you. It then shows how far your current savings get you and how many months it will take to build the rest.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Pick your currency.** The starting values change to suit it; anything you type is kept.
2. **Enter your monthly essential expenses:** rent or mortgage, food, bills, insurance and loan payments. Leave out things you'd cut in an emergency.
3. **Choose your income type** and enter how many people **depend on your income**.
4. **Check the months to cover.** It's filled in from the rule of thumb below; type your own number if you prefer.
5. **Enter what you've saved so far** and **how much you can save each month.**

The main result is your emergency fund target. Underneath you'll see how much is still to save, how long that will take and how far along you are.

## How much emergency fund do I need?

The calculator multiplies your essential monthly costs by the number of months you want covered:

```text
target         = monthly essentials × months to cover
still to save  = target − saved so far        (never below 0)
months to build = still to save ÷ monthly saving   (rounded up)
progress       = saved so far ÷ target
```

The number of months is the personal part. As **a common rule of thumb, not a rule**, the calculator suggests:

| Income | Suggested months |
|---|---|
| Steady salary | 3 |
| Variable or commission | 6 |
| Freelance or self-employed | 6 |

…plus one month for each person who depends on you, up to 12 months. These are starting points, not advice. If your job is very secure or you have other income, you might choose fewer months. If your industry is unstable or you have health costs, choose more.

## An emergency fund calculator for freelancers

Freelance income doesn't arrive evenly. Clients pay late, projects end without notice, and there's no sick pay or redundancy pay to fall back on. That's why the suggestion for self-employed income starts at six months rather than three.

A good emergency fund also makes you a better freelancer: you can turn down poorly paid work and negotiate from a calmer position. If your rate doesn't leave room to save, check it with the <ToolLink slug="hourly-rate-calculator">freelance hourly rate calculator</ToolLink>.

## Using it as an emergency savings calculator

Once you know your target, the question becomes how to get there. Enter a monthly amount you can save reliably and the calculator shows how many months it will take. Try a few amounts: saving a little more each month can cut the time noticeably.

## Worked examples

### 1. A US salaried worker

Taylor's essentials come to $3,000 a month. With a steady salary and nobody depending on them, the suggestion is 3 months. They've saved $2,000 and can add $500 a month.

- Emergency fund target: **$9,000.00**
- Still to save: **$7,000.00**
- Time to build it: **14 months**
- Progress: **22.22%**

**What this means for Taylor:** raising the monthly saving from $500 to $700 would get there in 10 months instead of 14.

### 2. An Indian freelancer with two dependants

Meera's essentials are ₹50,000 a month. As a freelancer with two dependants, the suggestion is 6 + 2 = 8 months. She has ₹1,00,000 saved and puts aside ₹20,000 a month.

- Emergency fund target: **₹4,00,000.00**
- Still to save: **₹3,00,000.00**
- Time to build it: **15 months**
- Progress: **25%**

**What this means for Meera:** a quarter of the way there already. A strong month for clients is a good time to add a lump sum.

### 3. A UK salesperson who is already covered

Sam's essentials are £2,000 a month and his income is mostly commission, so the suggestion is 6 months. He already has £15,000 saved.

- Emergency fund target: **£12,000.00**
- Still to save: **£0.00**: he's covered
- Progress: **125%**, which is **£3,000.00** more than the target

**What this means for Sam:** the extra £3,000 could go towards other goals, once he's sure 6 months is right for him.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Count essentials only.** The fund covers what you must pay, not your usual lifestyle.
- **Keep it separate** from your everyday account so it's not spent by accident.
- **Automate it.** A transfer on payday is easier than deciding every month.
- **Refill it after you use it.** That's what it's for.
- **Review it once a year,** or when your costs, job or family change.
