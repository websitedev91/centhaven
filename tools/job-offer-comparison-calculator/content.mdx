This job offer comparison calculator puts two offers side by side and gives each one a yearly value. It adds up salary, bonus, the employer's retirement contribution and other benefits, then takes off what the commute costs you. It also shows what each job really pays per hour once commute time is counted.

## How to use this compare two job offers calculator

1. **Pick your currency.**
2. **Fill in Offer A and Offer B** from the offer letters: base salary, target bonus, the employer's retirement contribution and any other benefits the employer pays for.
3. **Add the working pattern:** paid leave days, hours per week and how many days a week you would be in the office.
4. **Add the commute:** minutes each way and the round-trip cost per office day.
5. **Optional: value your commute time.** Enter what an hour of your free time is worth to you. Leave it at 0 to count only the money.

The main result tells you which offer is worth more per year and by how much. The side-by-side breakdown shows every line for both offers, including work hours, commute hours and effective pay per hour.

## How the comparison works

```text
work days     = 260 − paid leave days                (52 weeks × 5 days)
office days   = work days × office days per week ÷ 5
commute hours = office days × 2 × minutes one way ÷ 60
commute cost  = office days × cost per office day
total pay     = salary + bonus + retirement contribution + other benefits
yearly value  = total pay − commute cost − (commute hours × value of your time)
pay per hour  = (total pay − commute cost) ÷ (work hours + commute hours)
```

Everything is before tax. If both jobs are in the same country, tax usually affects them in a similar way, so the comparison still shows which offer is worth more. For take-home pay, check an official tax calculator.

## Job offer total compensation: what to count

Salary is only the start. Look for these in each offer letter:

- **Bonus:** use the target, and ask how often it has really been paid.
- **Retirement contribution:** what the employer adds, such as a 401(k) match, pension or provident fund. Check whether it vests over time.
- **Benefits:** health insurance the employer pays for, allowances, stipends and training budgets.
- **Paid leave:** more days off means fewer working hours for the same pay.

Stock, sign-on bonuses and career growth matter too. They are harder to put a yearly number on, so weigh them separately once you've compared the totals.

## Remote job offer comparison: the cost of the commute

A commute costs money and time. Five office days a week with 50 minutes each way is 400 hours a year, about ten full working weeks. Set office days to 0 for a remote job and the commute disappears from the comparison. If you want to count the time too, give it a value per hour.

## Worked examples

### 1. Hybrid job vs a higher salary in the office (US)

Maria has two offers. **Offer A** pays $85,000 with a 5% target bonus, a 4% retirement match, $6,000 of other benefits and 25 days of leave. She'd be in the office one day a week, 30 minutes each way, at $15 a day. **Offer B** pays $92,000 with no bonus, a 3% match, $3,000 of benefits and 20 days of leave, in the office five days a week, 50 minutes each way, at $15 a day.

- Offer A yearly value: **$97,945.00**
- Offer B yearly value: **$94,160.00**
- Offer A is worth **$3,785.00** more per year
- Pay per hour including commute: $50.83 for A, $40.59 for B

**What this means for Maria:** the $7,000 higher salary doesn't survive the bonus, benefits and commute. Offer A is worth more and pays far more per hour.

### 2. The same offers with commute time valued at $20 an hour

Maria decides an hour of her free time is worth $20 to her. Offer A has 47 commute hours a year; Offer B has 400.

- Offer A yearly value: **$97,005.00**
- Offer B yearly value: **$86,160.00**
- Offer A is worth **$10,845.00** more per year

**What this means for Maria:** once time counts, the gap almost triples.

### 3. Bigger package vs a remote job (India)

Arjun compares **Offer A**: ₹12,00,000 salary, a 10% bonus, a 12% employer retirement contribution and ₹50,000 of benefits, with 30 days of leave, 45 hours a week, in the office five days, an hour each way at ₹300 a day. **Offer B** is a remote job at ₹14,00,000 with no bonus, contribution or benefits, 20 days of leave and 45 hours a week.

- Offer A yearly value: **₹14,45,000.00**
- Offer B yearly value: **₹14,00,000.00**
- Offer A is worth **₹45,000.00** more per year
- Pay per hour including commute: ₹571.15 for A, ₹648.15 for B

**What this means for Arjun:** Offer A is worth more in total, but it costs him 460 hours of commuting a year. Per hour of his time, the remote job pays more. If he values his time at ₹100 an hour or more, Offer B comes out ahead.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Compare like with like.** Use yearly, before-tax figures for both offers.
- **Count real office days**, not the policy on paper.
- **Ask about the bonus history** and whether the retirement contribution vests.
