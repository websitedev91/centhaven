This 50/30/20 budget calculator splits your monthly take-home pay into three parts: needs, wants and savings. Start with the 50/30/20 split, change the percentages to fit your life, and compare the plan with what you really spend. The 50/30/20 split is a popular budgeting guideline, not a rule.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency** and enter your **monthly take-home pay**: what reaches your bank account after tax. We don't work out tax. The starting value is an example only.
2. **Keep or change the percentages.** They start at 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings, and need to total 100%.
3. **Compare with what you spend** (optional). Open the section and enter what you actually spend on needs and wants, and what you save, each month.

The result shows each part as a monthly and a yearly amount, a bar of the split, and, with actual spending, how far each part is over or under the plan.

## 50 30 20 rule calculator: how the split works

```text
needs   = take-home pay × needs % ÷ 100
wants   = take-home pay × wants % ÷ 100
savings = take-home pay × savings % ÷ 100

over or under = actual − planned
```

Needs are costs you must pay, such as rent, bills, food, transport and minimum debt payments. Wants are things you choose. Savings covers money you put aside and extra debt payments.

## Worked examples

### 1. The standard split

Someone takes home $4,000 a month.

- Needs (50%): **$2,000.00**
- Wants (30%): **$1,200.00**
- Savings (20%): **$800.00**, or $9,600 a year

### 2. More for needs

Someone takes home $3,500 a month and pays high rent, so they choose 60/30/10.

- Needs (60%): **$2,100.00**
- Wants (30%): **$1,050.00**
- Savings (10%): **$350.00**

**What this means:** a split you can keep is better than one you can't. They can raise savings later as rent or pay changes.

### 3. In rupees

Someone takes home ₹80,000 a month.

- Needs: **₹40,000.00**
- Wants: **₹24,000.00**
- Savings: **₹16,000.00**

## Needs, wants and savings: comparing with real spending

### 4. Plan against reality

The person taking home $4,000 checks their bank statement. They spend $2,300 on needs and $1,000 on wants, and save $700.

- Needs: **$300.00** over plan
- Wants: **$200.00** under plan
- Savings: **$100.00** less saved than planned

**What this means:** needs cost more than planned, and the money came out of savings. They can trim needs, move some wants money to savings, or change the plan to match their real costs.

## How to split my income when pay changes

If your pay goes up and down, use a lower month as your take-home pay. Budget from that, and put anything extra in a good month straight into savings. That way the plan still works in a quiet month.
