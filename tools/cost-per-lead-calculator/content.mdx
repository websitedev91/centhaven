This cost per lead calculator divides your campaign spend by the leads it brought in. Add your lead-to-customer rate and it also shows how many of those leads become customers and what each paying customer really costs. Use it to compare ads, channels and campaigns on the numbers that matter.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency** and enter your **campaign spend**. The starting values are examples only.
2. **Enter the leads** that spend brought in: enquiries, sign-ups or form fills.
3. **Add your lead-to-customer rate** if you know it. Out of 100 leads, how many buy? Leave it at 0 to skip.
4. **Compare two channels** to see which one brings customers more cheaply.

The result shows your cost per lead, and with a rate, the customers you can expect, the cost per customer and how many leads it takes to win one.

## How to calculate cost per lead

```text
cost per lead     = spend ÷ leads

customers         = leads × lead-to-customer rate
cost per customer = spend ÷ customers
leads per customer = 100 ÷ rate (%)
```

When the maths gives a part customer, the calculator shows it as an average, such as about 7.5 customers. Over many campaigns, that is what you would expect each time.

## CPL calculator: worked examples

### 1. A local service business

A plumber spends $2,000 on ads and gets 80 leads. One lead in ten books a job.

- Cost per lead: **$25.00**
- Customers: **8 customers**
- Cost per customer: **$250.00**

**What this means for the plumber:** each booked job needs to earn well over $250 to pay for the ads.

### 2. A business-to-business campaign

A UK firm spends £1,500 and gets 45 leads. One in five becomes a client.

- Cost per lead: **£33.33**
- Customers: **9 customers**
- Cost per customer: **£166.67**

### 3. A coaching business in rupees

A coach spends ₹60,000 and gets 150 leads. 5% sign up.

- Cost per lead: **₹400.00**
- Customers: **about 7.5 customers (on average)**
- Cost per customer: **₹8,000.00**

### 4. A small test with few leads

A test campaign spends $1,000 and gets 7 leads, with a 3% rate.

- Cost per lead: **$142.86**
- Customers: **about 0.21 customers (on average)**
- Cost per customer: **$4,761.90**

**What this means:** with so few leads, the test needs to run longer before the numbers mean much.

## Lead conversion rate calculator: why the rate matters

Two channels can have the same cost per lead and very different costs per customer. If one closes 20% of its leads and the other 5%, the first wins a customer for a quarter of the price. Small gains in your rate, from faster follow-up or a better offer, can cut what each customer costs as much as cheaper ads would.

## Tips

- **Agree what counts as a lead** and use it for every channel.
- **Compare by cost per customer.** Cheap leads that never buy are expensive.
- **Follow up fast.** Leads go cold quickly, and the rate falls with them.
- **Use enough data.** A handful of leads can swing the numbers a lot.
