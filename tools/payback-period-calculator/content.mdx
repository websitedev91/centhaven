This payback period calculator shows how long an investment takes to pay for itself. Enter what it costs up front and the cash it brings in, or saves, each year. If the cash flow changes from year to year, enter up to 10 years of cash flows and the calculator finds the point where they add up to the investment.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Pick a mode.** Same every year divides the investment by one yearly cash flow. Different each year adds the years up one by one.
2. **Choose your currency** and enter your **investment**: what you pay up front. The starting values are examples only.
3. **Enter the yearly cash flow**, or the number of years and the cash flow for each year.

The result shows the payback period in years and in years and months, a chart of the money recovered and a year-by-year table.

## How to calculate payback period

```text
same every year:
  payback = investment ÷ yearly cash flow

different each year:
  find the first year where the running total ≥ investment
  payback = (years before it)
            + (investment − total before that year) ÷ cash flow in that year

months = (payback − whole years) × 12, rounded
```

The calculator assumes the cash comes in evenly through each year. It works out simple payback, which does not discount later years.

## Investment payback calculator: worked examples

### 1. A machine that saves money

A workshop buys a machine for $50,000. It saves $12,500 a year in labour.

- Payback period: **4.00 years**
- That is **4 years and 0 months**

### 2. A shop fit-out in rupees

A shop spends ₹5,00,000 on a fit-out that brings in an extra ₹1,50,000 a year.

- Payback period: **3.33 years**
- That is **3 years and 4 months**

**What this means for the shop:** the fit-out has paid for itself a little after the third year. Anything after that is extra.

## Payback period with uneven cash flows

### 3. A new product line

A business invests $40,000. The cash flows are $10,000, $12,000, $15,000 and $18,000 over four years.

- After three years: $37,000 recovered, $3,000 still to go
- Year four brings $18,000, so the last $3,000 takes 3,000 ÷ 18,000 of a year
- Payback period: **3.17 years**, or **3 years and 2 months**
- Recovered by the end of year four: **$55,000.00**, which is $15,000.00 more than the investment

If the cash flows never add up to the investment within the years you enter, the calculator says how much was recovered and what share of the investment that is.

## What payback leaves out

Payback is quick and easy to explain, but it has two blind spots. It ignores what happens after the investment has paid for itself, so a project that keeps earning for years looks the same as one that stops. And it treats money in year five as worth the same as money today. Use it as a first check alongside other measures.

The calculator does not take negative cash flows, such as a year when the investment costs money to run. Enter 0 for that year, or reduce the other years by the same amount.
