This free invoice generator for freelancers creates a clean, professional invoice in your client's currency. Add your logo, your details and the client's, list the work, add a discount, a tax line or a deposit already paid, and download the invoice as a PDF. There's no sign-up, and your invoice is saved only in your browser.

## How to use this freelance invoice template

1. **Pick the currency** you're billing in, then check the invoice number, issue date and payment terms. The due date is worked out for you.
2. **Add your logo and details:** name or business, address, email, phone and any tax or business ID.
3. **Add the client** under Bill to.
4. **List the work as line items:** description, quantity or hours, and rate. Add, remove or reorder lines with the buttons.
5. **Optional: add a discount, a tax line or an amount already paid.** The tax line starts at 0%: enter the label and rate that apply to you.
6. **Add your payment details** (bank account, IBAN/SWIFT, or a PayPal or Wise email) and any notes.
7. **Click Download PDF** and choose **Save as PDF** in the print window. Only the invoice is printed.

After you send it, **Start a new invoice** keeps your details, logo, payment details and terms, clears the client and lines, and suggests the next number (INV-0001 becomes INV-0002). **Clear form** deletes everything saved on this device.

## How the invoice total is worked out

```text
line amount  = quantity × rate                    (rounded to the cent)
subtotal     = sum of the line amounts
discount     = subtotal × discount %
tax          = (subtotal − discount) × tax %
total        = subtotal − discount + tax
balance due  = total − amount already paid
due date     = issue date + payment terms (days)
```

Each amount is rounded to the cent before it's added up, so the numbers on the PDF always add up exactly.

## Multi currency invoice generator: billing clients abroad

Choose US dollars, pounds, euros, Canadian or Australian dollars, or rupees, and every amount on the invoice uses that currency, with Indian digit grouping for rupees. Before you bill a client abroad, agree the currency and check what it costs you to receive the money. Transfer and conversion fees can take a real share of a payment, so put payment details on the invoice that suit the currency.

## Invoice generator with GST, VAT or sales tax

You can add one tax line with your own label (such as GST, VAT or Sales tax) and your own rate. The generator never suggests a rate, because the right one depends on where you are, what you sell and whether you're registered.

This is a simple invoice. Some countries require extra details on tax invoices (for example GST in India or VAT in the UK/EU). Check your local rules.

## Worked examples

### 1. A US web designer billing the balance after a deposit

Nora built a website for $2,400 and added three extra pages at $150 each. The client paid a $1,000 deposit at the start. Her terms are Net 14 from 18 November 2026.

- Total: **$2,850.00**
- Amount paid: $1,000.00
- Balance due: **$1,850.00** by 2 December 2026

**What this means for Nora:** showing the deposit on the invoice makes the balance clear, so the client pays the right amount without asking.

### 2. A UK consultant with a tax line

Tom bills 12.5 hours of consulting at £80 and £45.60 of travel. He's registered for tax and enters his 20% rate himself. Payment is due on receipt.

- Subtotal: £1,045.60
- Tax (20%): £209.12
- Total: **£1,254.72**

**What this means for Tom:** half hours are fine: 12.5 × £80 is exactly £1,000.00, and every line is rounded to the penny, so the totals match the lines.

### 3. An Indian developer paid in full

Ravi invoices two milestones of app development at ₹25,000 each, with a 10% discount and a 5% tax line he entered. The client has already paid ₹47,250. The invoice is dated 15 December 2026 on Net 30 terms.

- Discount: ₹5,000.00
- Tax (5%): ₹2,250.00
- Total: **₹47,250.00**
- Balance due: ₹0.00, shown as **Paid in full**

**What this means for Ravi:** a paid-in-full invoice is a useful record for the client's accounts and for his own.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Number invoices in order** and never reuse a number.
- **Write the due date as a date**, not just "Net 30".
- **Put your payment details on every invoice**, so the client never has to ask.
- **Send it as a PDF**, not an editable file.
