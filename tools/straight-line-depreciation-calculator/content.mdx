This straight-line depreciation calculator shows how much an asset loses in value each year, and builds a full year-by-year schedule of depreciation and book value. It works for laptops, vehicles, machinery, furniture and any other equipment, including assets bought part-way through the year.

**Accounting method, not tax depreciation.** This is the straight-line method used in your own accounts and plans. Tax depreciation follows each country's own rules (for example MACRS in the US, capital allowances in the UK or written-down value rates in India), and the amounts can be quite different. Check the tax side with an accountant.

## How to use this depreciation schedule calculator

1. **Pick your currency.** The starting values change to suit it; anything you type is kept.
2. **Enter the asset cost:** what you paid, including delivery and setup.
3. **Enter the salvage value:** what it will be worth when you stop using it. Use zero if nothing.
4. **Enter the useful life** in whole years.
5. **Set the months used in the first year.** Keep 12 if you bought it at the start of your year; use fewer for a mid-year purchase.

The main result is the depreciation per year. Underneath you'll see the monthly amount, the yearly rate and a schedule showing the book value at the end of every year. Switch the schedule to monthly to see every month, and download either view as a CSV file for your spreadsheet or accountant.

## How straight-line depreciation is calculated

Take the cost, subtract the salvage value, and spread what's left evenly over the useful life.

```text
depreciable amount = cost − salvage value
yearly depreciation = depreciable amount ÷ useful life (years)
monthly depreciation = yearly depreciation ÷ 12
book value = cost − depreciation so far
```

If the asset was bought part-way through the year, the first year only takes the months it was used, and the rest moves to one extra year at the end. The total is the same; it's just spread over the real time you own it.

## Equipment depreciation calculator: what counts as cost

The cost isn't only the price on the receipt. Include anything you paid to get the asset ready to use, such as delivery, installation and setup. Don't include running costs like fuel, repairs, software subscriptions or insurance; those are normal expenses of the year you pay them.

The salvage value is your honest estimate of what the asset will be worth at the end, for example its second-hand price. The book value never falls below it.

## Worked examples

### 1. A freelancer's laptop

Alex buys a laptop for $1,500 and expects to sell it for $300 after 3 years. It's used for the whole first year.

- Depreciation per year: **$400.00** (**$33.33** a month)
- Yearly rate: **33.33%** of the depreciable amount
- Book value after year 1: **$1,100.00** · year 2: **$700.00** · year 3: **$300.00**

**What this means for Alex:** the laptop costs the business $400 a year in value, which is worth knowing when you work out your hourly rate.

### 2. A UK van bought mid-year

A small business buys a van for £50,000, expects it to be worth £5,000 after 5 years, and starts using it 6 months into the year.

- Depreciation per full year: **£9,000.00**
- Year 1 (6 months): **£4,500.00**, book value **£45,500.00**
- Years 2 to 5: **£9,000.00** a year
- Year 6 (the last 6 months): **£4,500.00**, final book value **£5,000.00**

**What this means:** buying mid-year doesn't change the total, only when it's recorded. The schedule runs into a sixth year to cover the full 5 years of use.

### 3. Office equipment in India

An office buys equipment for ₹1,00,000, expects it to be worth nothing after 4 years, and uses it from the start of the year.

- Depreciation per year: **₹25,000.00** (**₹2,083.33** a month)
- Book value after each year: **₹75,000.00**, **₹50,000.00**, **₹25,000.00**, **₹0.00**

**What this means:** a quarter of the cost is written off each year. Remember that India's tax depreciation uses its own rates, so the tax figures will differ.

## Laptop depreciation and other short-lived assets

Laptops, phones and tablets lose value quickly and are often replaced within a few years, so a short useful life and a small salvage value usually fit best. Vans, machinery and furniture tend to last longer. Use your own replacement habits rather than a rule of thumb.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Use a realistic useful life:** how long you'll keep it, not how long it could last.
- **Don't forget salvage value.** Setting it to zero when the asset will still sell for something overstates depreciation.
- **Keep running costs out.** Repairs, fuel and subscriptions aren't part of the asset's cost.
- **Keep the tax figures separate.** Tax rules differ by country; your accountant will handle them.
- **Use it in your pricing.** Equipment wears out; make sure your rates cover it, and check your monthly sales target with the <ToolLink slug="break-even-calculator">break even calculator</ToolLink>.
