This Upwork fee calculator shows the service fee on a contract and how much you take home. It also works backwards: enter what you want to keep, and it shows the price you need to charge. The fee percentage always comes from you, so use the one shown on your own proposal or offer.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose a mode.** Pick **I'm charging** if you know the contract amount, or **I want to take home** if you know what you need to keep.
2. **Enter the amount in US dollars.**
3. **Enter your service fee %.** Use the percentage shown on your proposal or offer. The calculator starts at 10% as an example only.

The result shows your take-home or the price to charge, with the fee in dollars underneath. The bar below splits the contract into what you keep and what goes in fees.

## How the Upwork service fee calculator works

The fee is a percentage of the contract amount. Working backwards divides by what is left after the fee:

```text
fee        = contract amount × fee %
take-home  = contract amount − fee

price to charge = amount you want ÷ (1 − fee %)
```

For example, to take home $20 with a 10% fee, charge $20 ÷ 0.9 = **$22.22**. The fee is $2.22, and $20 reaches you.

## How much does Upwork take?

The honest answer is: check your proposal. The percentage can differ from contract to contract, and platforms change their pricing, so this page doesn't quote a number. The fee Upwork shows you before you commit is the one that applies. Enter it here and you'll see exactly what it means in dollars.

## Upwork fees for freelancers: worked examples

These examples use made-up fee percentages to show the maths. Use your own.

### 1. A developer's fixed-price job

Alex, a developer in the US, wins a $1,000 fixed-price job. The offer shows a 10% service fee.

- Service fee: **$100.00**
- Alex takes home: **$900.00**

**What this means for Alex:** to keep a full $1,000, Alex would need to quote $1,111.11 next time.

### 2. A designer who knows what she needs

Priya, a designer in India, needs to keep $850 from a logo project. Her proposal shows a 15% fee.

- Price to charge: **$1,000.00**
- Service fee: **$150.00**
- Priya takes home: **$850.00**

**What this means for Priya:** quoting $850 would have left her $722.50, so starting from her target saved her $127.50.

### 3. An hourly rate after fees

Sam wants $20 an hour after a 10% fee.

- Hourly rate to set: **$22.22**
- Fee per hour: **$2.22**

**What this means for Sam:** over a 30-hour week, the higher rate keeps $600 in Sam's pocket instead of $540.

## Tips for keeping more of each contract

- **Price from your take-home, not the other way round.** Use the **I want to take home** mode before you send a proposal.
- **Check the fee on every proposal.** Don't assume last month's percentage still applies.
- **Count every cost of winning work.** Proposal costs and withdrawal charges come on top of the service fee, so check them in your account.
