This CAC calculator shows what it costs you to win one new customer. Enter your marketing spend, any sales spend and the new customers you won in the same period. Add what a customer is worth to you over time and it also shows your LTV:CAC ratio, so you can see whether each customer pays back what it cost to win them.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency** and enter your **marketing spend** for a period, such as a month. The starting values are examples only.
2. **Add your sales spend** if you have any: sales salaries, commissions and tools. Leave it at 0 to see marketing alone.
3. **Enter your new customers** for the same period.
4. **Add a customer lifetime value** if you know it, to see the LTV:CAC section. Leave it at 0 to skip.
5. **Set a target ratio** if you want one. It starts at 3, a common rule of thumb, not a rule.

The result shows your customer acquisition cost, your total spend, and with a lifetime value, the ratio, what is left after the acquisition cost and the highest CAC that meets your target.

## How the customer acquisition cost calculator works

```text
total spend = marketing spend + sales spend
CAC         = total spend ÷ new customers

LTV:CAC ratio = lifetime value ÷ CAC
left over     = lifetime value − CAC
highest CAC for a target = lifetime value ÷ target ratio
```

If your ratio is below the target, the calculator also shows how many new customers you would need to win with the same spend to reach it.

## How to calculate customer acquisition cost: worked examples

### 1. An online shop

A shop spends $8,000 on marketing and $4,000 on sales in a month, and wins 60 new customers. Each customer is worth $900 over time.

- Total spend: **$12,000.00**
- CAC: **$200.00**
- LTV:CAC: **4.5 : 1**
- Left after acquisition cost: **$700.00**

**What this means for the shop:** it is above a 3 : 1 target, so it could afford to spend more to grow.

### 2. A software business below its target

A small software company spends £5,000 on marketing in a month and wins 40 customers worth £300 each.

- CAC: **£125.00**
- LTV:CAC: **2.4 : 1**
- Highest CAC for 3 : 1: **£100.00**
- Customers needed for the same spend: **50**

**What this means for the company:** it needs 10 more customers a month from the same budget, or a lower spend, to reach its target.

### 3. An agency in rupees

An agency spends ₹4,00,000 on marketing and ₹2,00,000 on sales, and wins 150 clients worth ₹18,000 each.

- Total spend: **₹6,00,000.00**
- CAC: **₹4,000.00**
- LTV:CAC: **4.5 : 1**

### 4. When customers cost more than they bring in

A business spends $10,000 and wins 20 customers worth $400 each.

- CAC: **$500.00**
- LTV:CAC: **0.8 : 1**
- Left after acquisition cost: **-$100.00**

**What this means:** every new customer loses money. It would need 75 customers for the same spend to reach 3 : 1.

## LTV to CAC ratio calculator: reading the result

The ratio compares what a customer brings in with what they cost to win. A ratio of 1 : 1 means you only get your money back. Below that, each new customer costs more than they are worth.

A higher ratio is not always better. A very high ratio can mean you are spending too little and growing more slowly than you could. That is why the target is yours to set. Many people start from 3 : 1, but your margins and cash decide what works.

## Cost to acquire a customer: tips

- **Use the same period** for spend and new customers.
- **Count only new customers.** Repeat orders from existing customers do not belong in CAC.
- **Use profit for lifetime value** where you can. Revenue makes the ratio look better than it is.

- **Know your break-even.** The <ToolLink slug="break-even-calculator">break even calculator</ToolLink> shows how many customers cover your fixed costs.
