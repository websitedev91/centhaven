This savings rate calculator shows the share of your income you save each month. Enter your monthly take-home pay or gross income and what you save, and it gives your savings rate as a percentage, what that adds up to in a year, and what an optional target of your own would mean. Every number comes from you, and we never work out tax.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your income basis.** Take-home pay, what reaches your bank account, or Gross income, before tax. It only changes the labels; you type the number either way.
2. **Choose your currency** and enter your **monthly income**.
3. **Enter your monthly savings**: everything you put aside, including investments and, if you choose, extra debt payments.
4. **Add a target**, if you have one of your own. Leave it at 0 to skip it.

The starting values are examples only.

## How the savings rate is worked out

```text
savings rate   = monthly savings ÷ monthly income × 100
saved per year = monthly savings × 12
target amount  = monthly income × your target % ÷ 100
gap            = target amount − monthly savings
```

## Savings percentage calculator: worked examples

### 1. Take-home pay

You take home $4,000 a month and save $600.

- Savings rate: 600 ÷ 4,000 = **15%**
- Saved each year: **$7,200.00**
- Not saved each month: **$3,400.00**

**What this means:** 15 cents of every dollar you take home goes into savings.

### 2. With a target of your own

Same numbers, with your own target of 20%.

- Target amount: 20% of 4,000 = **$800.00** a month
- Gap: **$200.00 more** a month to reach 20%

**What this means:** saving an extra $200 a month would meet the target you set.

### 3. In rupees

You take home ₹80,000 a month and save ₹12,000, with a target of 25%.

- Savings rate: **15%**
- Saved each year: **₹1,44,000.00**
- Target amount: **₹20,000.00**, which is **₹8,000.00 more** a month

## Personal savings rate calculator: take-home or gross?

Both are fair, as long as you use the same one each time. Take-home pay shows the share of money you can actually use. Gross income is steadier when tax changes, and easier to compare across jobs. The same $600 is 15% of $4,000 take-home pay but 7.62% of $5,250 gross income. The calculator lets you choose and never converts one into the other.

## Percent of income saved: tracking it over time

A savings rate is most useful when you compare it with your own past months. Use Compare two amounts under the calculator to see how a bigger monthly saving would change your rate, for example $600 against $800. Small, steady changes add up: an extra $200 a month is $2,400 a year.
