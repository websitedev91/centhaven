This late fee calculator works out the fee on an overdue invoice from the terms you agreed with your client, and the new total they owe. Choose a flat fee, a one-off percentage or a percentage per 30 days charged by the day, and add a grace period if your terms include one.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency** and enter the **invoice amount**.
2. **Enter how many days late** the payment is, counting from the due date.
3. **Add a grace period** if your terms give one. Leave it at 0 otherwise.
4. **Choose your type of late fee** and enter it exactly as your contract or invoice terms state it. The starting values are examples only.

The result shows the late fee, the days charged and the new total due.

## How the invoice late fee calculator works

```text
days charged  = days late − grace period   (never below 0)

flat fee      = the flat fee, once the invoice is late
one-off %     = invoice amount × fee %
% per 30 days = invoice amount × fee % × days charged ÷ 30

new total     = invoice amount + late fee
```

## Late payment fee calculator: worked examples

These examples use made-up fees. Use the fee in your own terms.

Nina's $2,000 invoice is 45 days late. Her terms say 2% per 30 days.

- New total: **$2,060.00**

**What this means for Nina:** each extra day adds about $1.33.

## How to charge a late fee on an invoice

- **Agree it first.** Put the late fee in your contract or proposal before the work starts.
- **Show it on every invoice.** A short line such as "late payments incur the fee in our terms" sets clear expectations.
- **Remind before you charge.** A friendly reminder often gets the invoice paid without any fee.
