This contribution margin calculator shows what each sale adds after its variable costs. Enter your price, variable cost per unit and units sold to see the margin per unit, the contribution margin ratio and the total. Add your fixed costs to see how many units you need to break even, and your profit or loss. Every number comes from you.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency** and enter your **price per unit**.
2. **Enter the variable cost per unit**: everything that goes up with each sale, such as materials, packaging and per-sale fees.
3. **Enter the units sold** for a period you choose, such as a month.
4. **Add fixed costs** for the same period, if you want the break-even point and profit. Leave it at 0 to skip them.

The starting values are examples only.

## How to calculate contribution margin

```text
contribution margin per unit = price − variable cost per unit
contribution margin ratio    = margin per unit ÷ price × 100
total contribution margin    = margin per unit × units sold
break-even units             = fixed costs ÷ margin per unit   (rounded up)
break-even revenue           = fixed costs ÷ ratio
profit                       = total contribution margin − fixed costs
```

## Contribution margin per unit: worked examples

### 1. A simple product

You sell at $50, each unit costs $30 in variable costs, you sell 1,000 a month and your fixed costs are $12,000.

- Margin per unit: **$20.00**
- Ratio: **40%**
- Total: **$20,000.00**
- Break-even: **600 units**, or **$30,000.00** of sales
- Profit: **$8,000.00**

**What this means:** every sale brings in $20 towards your fixed costs; after the 600th sale, each one adds $20 of profit.

### 2. A lower price point

You sell at $19.99 with $7.45 of variable cost, 2,500 units, and $10,000 of fixed costs.

- Margin per unit: **$12.54**
- Ratio: **62.73%**
- Total: **$31,350.00**
- Break-even: **798 units** (797.45 exactly, rounded up)

### 3. When the margin is negative

You sell at $50, but each unit costs $55.

- Margin per unit: **−$5.00**, a ratio of **−10%**
- 1,000 units: **−$5,000.00**

**What this means:** every sale loses money, so there is no break-even point. Raising the price or cutting the variable cost is the only way out of it.

## Contribution margin ratio calculator: comparing products

The ratio shows what share of each sale is left after variable costs, so you can compare products with different prices. A $10 item with a $6 margin has a 60% ratio; a $100 item with a $30 margin has a 30% ratio. Use Compare two prices under the calculator to see how a price change moves the margin, the ratio and your break-even point.

## Tips

- **Keep periods the same.** Use fixed costs and units sold for the same month or year.
- **Count every variable cost.** Fees and shipping per sale are easy to forget.
