This food cost calculator shows how much of a dish's menu price goes on ingredients. You can work out the food cost percentage of a dish, set a menu price from your own target, or find your food cost for a week or month from your stock and sales. Every number, including the target, comes from you.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Pick a mode.** Food cost % checks a dish you already sell. Menu price works out a price from a target. For a period uses your stock count and sales.
2. **Choose your currency** and enter the **ingredient cost** of one portion, then the **menu price** or your **target food cost**. The starting values are examples only.
3. **For a period,** enter your opening inventory, purchases, closing inventory and food sales for the same dates.

The result shows the food cost percentage or menu price, the gross profit per plate and a bar showing how the price splits between ingredients and profit.

## How to calculate food cost percentage

```text
food cost %        = ingredient cost ÷ menu price × 100
gross profit       = menu price − ingredient cost

menu price         = ingredient cost ÷ (target food cost % ÷ 100)

cost of food used  = opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory
period food cost % = cost of food used ÷ food sales × 100
```

Use menu prices and food sales before any sales tax you add on top.

## Worked examples

### 1. One dish

A café's pasta uses $3.50 of ingredients and sells for $12.00.

- Food cost: **29.17%**
- Gross profit per plate: **$8.50**, before labour and other costs

**What this means:** about 29 cents of every dollar on the menu price pays for the ingredients.

## Menu price calculator: pricing a new dish

### 2. From a target

A new dish uses $4.00 of ingredients, and the owner's own target food cost is 32%.

- Menu price: **$12.50** (4.00 ÷ 0.32)
- Gross profit per plate: $8.50

### 3. In rupees

A dish uses ₹120 of ingredients, with a 32% target.

- Menu price: **₹375.00**

**What this means:** the target sets how much room each price leaves for wages, rent and profit. Round the result to a price that suits your menu.

## Food cost percentage calculator for a week or month

### 4. A month of trading

A restaurant starts the month with $4,000 of food in stock, buys $12,000 more and ends with $3,500. Food sales for the month are $40,000.

- Cost of food used: **$12,500.00**
- Food cost for the month: **31.25%**

**What this means:** compare this with your recipe food costs. A gap points to waste, spoilage, staff meals or portions that drift from the recipe.

## Plate cost calculator: costing a recipe

To find the ingredient cost of one portion, list each ingredient, the amount used and what that amount costs you now. Add them up and divide by the number of portions the recipe makes. Re-cost your recipes when your supplier prices change, because an old cost hides a rising food cost.

## Tips

- **Set your own target.** Base it on your prices and your other costs, not on someone else's number.
- **Count stock the same way each time.** Same day, same method, same prices.
- **See margin and markup.** The <ToolLink slug="margin-vs-markup-calculator">margin vs markup calculator</ToolLink> shows the same price as margin and markup.
- **Cover your fixed costs.** The <ToolLink slug="break-even-calculator">break even calculator</ToolLink> shows how many covers you need each month.
- **Count card fees too.** The <ToolLink slug="payment-processing-fee-calculator">payment processing fee calculator</ToolLink> shows what each sale costs in fees.
