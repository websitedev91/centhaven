This current ratio calculator compares what your business owns and expects to turn into cash within a year with what it owes in that time. Enter your current assets and current liabilities, and optionally your inventory and prepaid expenses, to see the current ratio, the quick ratio and your working capital. It uses only the numbers from your own balance sheet.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency.** It only changes how amounts are shown; the ratios stay the same.
2. **Enter your current assets**: cash, money customers owe you, stock and anything else you expect to turn into cash within a year.
3. **Enter your current liabilities**: bills, short-term loans, wages and taxes owed, and anything else due within a year.
4. **Add inventory and prepaid expenses** if you have them. They are taken out for the quick ratio. Leave them at 0 if you have none.

The starting values are examples only. Use the figures from the same balance sheet date, so assets and liabilities line up.

## How to calculate current ratio

```text
current ratio   = current assets ÷ current liabilities
quick assets    = current assets − inventory − prepaid expenses
quick ratio     = quick assets ÷ current liabilities
working capital = current assets − current liabilities
```

A ratio of 2.00 means there are 2 of current assets for every 1 of current liabilities. A ratio of 1.00 means they are equal, and below 1.00 means current liabilities are the larger of the two.

## Quick ratio calculator: worked examples

### 1. A business with stock

Current assets are $250,000, including $60,000 of inventory and $10,000 of prepaid rent. Current liabilities are $125,000.

- Current ratio: 250,000 ÷ 125,000 = **2.00**
- Quick assets: 250,000 − 60,000 − 10,000 = **$180,000.00**
- Quick ratio: 180,000 ÷ 125,000 = **1.44**
- Working capital: **$125,000.00**

**What this means:** there are $2.00 of current assets for every $1 owed within a year, and $1.44 once stock and prepayments are left out.

### 2. Liabilities larger than assets

Current assets are $90,000, including $30,000 of inventory. Current liabilities are $120,000.

- Current ratio: **0.75**
- Quick ratio: 60,000 ÷ 120,000 = **0.50**
- Working capital: **−$30,000.00**

**What this means:** the debts due within a year are $30,000 more than the current assets. The calculator shows that gap; what you do about it depends on your own cash flow.

### 3. In rupees

Current assets are ₹50,00,000, with ₹12,00,000 of inventory and ₹2,00,000 prepaid. Current liabilities are ₹25,00,000.

- Current ratio: **2.00**
- Quick ratio: **1.44**
- Working capital: **₹25,00,000.00**

## Acid test ratio: what the quick ratio leaves out

The quick ratio is also called the acid test ratio. It leaves out inventory, because stock has to be sold before it becomes cash, and prepaid expenses, because money already paid for rent or insurance can’t be spent again. Some books work it out the other way round, as cash plus short-term investments plus receivables, divided by current liabilities. When those are the only other current assets, both ways give the same answer.

## Working capital calculator: the same numbers as an amount

Working capital uses the same two figures as the current ratio, but subtracts instead of dividing. The ratio is useful for comparing businesses of different sizes; the amount shows how much money sits between what you own and what you owe in the short term. A negative amount means current liabilities are larger.
