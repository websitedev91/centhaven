This utilization rate calculator shows what share of your available hours you actually bill, how far that is from your target, and what the gap is worth in revenue. It works for a freelancer's week, a designer's month or a whole consulting team's year.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose a period:** a week, a month or a year. It only changes the labels, so use the same period for every number.
2. **Enter your billable hours.** These are hours a client pays for.
3. **Enter your available hours.** This is all the time you, or the team, could work in that period. If some of it is leave, enter those hours under **Leave/holiday hours to exclude**.
4. **Set your target.** Many agencies aim for somewhere around 70–80%, but that's a common range, not a rule.
5. **Add your hourly rate** if you want to see what the gap is worth. Leave it at zero to skip the revenue gap.

The result shows your utilization rate. The bar underneath splits your hours into billable and non-billable, with a line at your target.

## How to calculate utilization rate

Utilization is billable hours divided by available hours. The calculator then compares that with your target:

```text
net available = available hours − leave hours
utilization   = billable hours ÷ net available
target hours  = target × available hours
gap           = target hours − billable hours   (negative = above target)
revenue gap   = gap × hourly rate               (0 if at or above target)
non-billable  = available hours − billable hours
```

So 28 billable hours out of 40 is 70%. With a 75% target you'd want 30 billable hours, which leaves a gap of 2 hours.

## Gross vs net capacity

**Gross capacity** is every hour you could work in the period, such as 40 hours in a normal week. **Net capacity** is what is left after leave: holidays, sick days and public holidays. Measure utilization against net capacity, otherwise a holiday week looks like a quiet week.

For example, 28 billable hours in a 40-hour week is 70%. If 8 of those 40 hours were a public holiday, net capacity is 32 hours and the same 28 billable hours are 87.5%, comfortably above a 75% target.

## Billable hours vs non-billable hours

Billable hours are the ones you can put on an invoice. Non-billable hours still matter: sales calls, proposals, invoicing, email, learning and marketing all keep the business running, they just don't get paid directly.

The aim isn't to remove non-billable time. It's to know how much there is, keep it in proportion and make sure your rate covers it. If you can only bill 70% of your time, your hourly rate has to earn a full week's income in 70% of the week.

## Using it as a billable utilization calculator for a team

For a team, add up everyone's billable hours and everyone's available hours for the same period. The result is the team's billable utilization. Agencies often look at it person by person too, because a team average can hide one person who is overloaded and another who is waiting for work.

## Worked examples

### 1. A freelancer's week

Sam could work 40 hours this week and billed 28. Sam's target is 75% and the rate is $100 an hour.

- Utilization: **70%**
- Billable hours at target: **30**
- Gap: **2** hours below target
- Revenue gap: **$200.00** this week
- Non-billable hours: **12**

**What this means for Sam:** two more billable hours a week is worth about $10,000 over a 50-week year.

### 2. An agency designer's month

Chloe has 160 available hours this month and billed 120. Her agency's target is 80% and her time is charged at £75 an hour.

- Utilization: **75%**
- Billable hours at target: **128**
- Gap: **8** hours below target
- Revenue gap: **£600.00** this month
- Non-billable hours: **40**

**What this means for Chloe:** one quiet day a month is the whole gap. Moving internal meetings into fewer, shorter slots could close it.

### 3. A consulting team's year

A small consulting team had 1,800 available hours last year and billed 1,200 of them. Their target is 65% and their blended rate is ₹1,500 an hour.

- Utilization: **66.67%**
- Billable hours at target: **1,170**
- **30** hours above target
- Revenue gap: **₹0.00**
- Non-billable hours: **600**

**What this means for the team:** they're slightly above target. If that continues, they may need more people, or a higher rate, before the team burns out.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Use the same period** for billable and available hours. Mixing a month of billable hours with a week of available hours gives nonsense.
- **Count available hours honestly.** Leave out holidays and public holidays you don't work.
- **Don't aim for 100%.** Without time for sales and admin, next month's billable hours dry up.
- **Track for a few weeks** before setting a target. One week can be unusually busy or quiet.
- **Fix your rate if your utilization is low.** Use the <ToolLink slug="hourly-rate-calculator">freelance hourly rate calculator</ToolLink> to set a rate that works at your real billable time.
