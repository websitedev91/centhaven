This ROAS calculator shows how much revenue your ads bring back for each unit you spend, as a ratio and a percentage. Add your profit margin to see your break-even ROAS and the profit left after ad spend. Or switch to Revenue needed to see what sales a target ROAS requires.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Pick a mode.** Find my ROAS works out the return from your results. Revenue needed works backwards from a target.
2. **Choose your currency** and enter your **revenue from ads** and your **ad spend** for the same period. The starting values are examples only.
3. **Enter a target ROAS** in Revenue needed mode. 4 means 4 in revenue for every 1 spent.
4. **Add your profit margin** before ad costs if you know it. Leave it at 0 to skip.

The result shows your ROAS, and with a margin, your break-even ROAS, your profit after ad spend and whether the ads are above or below break-even.

## How to calculate ROAS

```text
ROAS            = revenue from ads ÷ ad spend
ROAS %          = ROAS × 100

break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ profit margin
profit after ads = revenue × profit margin − ad spend

revenue needed  = ad spend × target ROAS
```

The profit margin here is what is left of each sale after product and delivery costs, before any ad costs.

## Return on ad spend calculator: worked examples

### 1. A profitable campaign

A shop spends $3,000 on ads and the ads bring in $15,000 of sales. Its margin before ads is 40%.

- ROAS: **5.00x**, or **500%**
- Break-even ROAS: **2.50x**
- Profit after ad spend: **$3,000.00**

**What this means for the shop:** the ads are well above break-even, so they add profit.

### 2. A campaign below break-even

A brand spends $2,500 on ads and they bring in $4,000. Its margin is 25%.

- ROAS: **1.60x**, or **160%**
- Break-even ROAS: **4.00x**
- Profit after ad spend: **-$1,500.00**

**What this means for the brand:** with a thin margin, it needs four times its spend back just to break even.

### 3. A shop in rupees

A shop spends ₹50,000 on ads and they bring in ₹2,50,000. Its margin is 30%.

- ROAS: **5.00x**
- Break-even ROAS: **3.33x**, or **333.33%**
- Profit after ad spend: **₹25,000.00**

## Target ROAS calculator: planning a budget

### 4. Revenue for a target

A business plans to spend $3,000 and wants a ROAS of 4x.

- Revenue needed: **$12,000.00**

If its margin is only 20%, its break-even ROAS is **5.00x**. A 4x target would lose money, and the calculator warns about it.

## Break even ROAS calculator: why margin matters

Two businesses can run the same ads with the same ROAS and get opposite results. A 3x ROAS is profitable at a 50% margin, where break-even is 2x. At a 25% margin, where break-even is 4x, it loses money. That is why the calculator asks for your own margin instead of quoting a typical ROAS.

## Tips

- **Match the period and channel.** Compare revenue and spend from the same campaign and dates.
- **Check profit, not just ROAS.** A high ROAS can still lose money on a thin margin.
- **Set your target above break-even.** Leave room for costs that are not in the ads.
- **Look at the whole cost of a customer.** The <ToolLink slug="cac-calculator">CAC calculator</ToolLink> adds sales and other marketing costs.
- **Compare channels.** The <ToolLink slug="cost-per-lead-calculator">cost per lead calculator</ToolLink> shows what each enquiry and customer costs.
- **Cover your fixed costs.** The <ToolLink slug="break-even-calculator">break even calculator</ToolLink> shows how many sales you need each month.
