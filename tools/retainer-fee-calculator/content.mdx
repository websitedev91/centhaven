This retainer fee calculator prices a monthly retainer from your hourly rate, the hours you commit and any discount. It also shows what the retainer is really worth per hour if the client uses fewer hours than agreed, so you can quote with confidence.

## How to use this monthly retainer pricing calculator

1. **Pick your currency** and enter your **normal hourly rate**.
2. **Enter the hours you'll commit each month.** This is time you keep free for the client.
3. **Set a discount** if you offer one for guaranteed work. Zero is fine.
4. **Enter the agreement length** in months.
5. **Estimate how many of those hours the client will actually use.** This drives the effective hourly rate.

The main result is the monthly fee. Underneath you'll see the same hours billed one by one, the discount you're giving, the total agreement value and your effective hourly rate.

## How the retainer fee is calculated

```text
hourly billing = hourly rate × hours per month
monthly fee    = hourly billing × (1 − discount)
discount given = hourly billing × discount
agreement value = monthly fee × months
effective rate = monthly fee ÷ hours actually used
```

The effective rate is the number most quotes miss. If a client pays for 20 hours but uses 16, every hour you work earns more than your normal rate. If they use all 20, your effective rate is your discounted rate.

## How to price a retainer

Start from the hourly rate you already charge. If you're not sure it's high enough, check it with the <ToolLink slug="hourly-rate-calculator">freelance hourly rate calculator</ToolLink> first: a retainer locks a rate in for months.

Then decide what the guaranteed income is worth to you. A retainer saves you time on sales and invoicing and smooths out your income, so a small discount can be fair. But you're also holding time aside, which has value of its own. Some freelancers give no discount at all and offer faster response times instead.

## Retainer agreement pricing: what to put in writing

The price only works if the terms are clear. Write down:

- the hours or deliverables included each month
- what kind of work is covered, and what isn't
- how quickly you'll respond and how requests are made
- what happens to unused hours: they expire, or a few roll over
- the payment date (usually the start of the month) and the notice period

Without these, a retainer slowly turns into unlimited work for a fixed fee.

## Worked examples

### 1. A US marketing freelancer

Jess charges $100 an hour. A client wants 20 hours a month for 6 months, and she offers a 10% discount. She expects them to use about 80% of the hours.

- Monthly retainer fee: **$1,800.00**
- Same hours billed hourly: **$2,000.00**
- Discount given: **$200.00** a month
- Agreement value: **$10,800.00**
- Effective rate at 80% use: **$112.50** an hour

**What this means for Jess:** even with the discount, she earns more per hour worked than her normal rate, as long as the client doesn't use every hour.

### 2. An Indian developer on a 12-month retainer

Kiran charges ₹2,000 an hour and agrees to 30 hours a month for a year at 15% off. The client uses every hour.

- Monthly retainer fee: **₹51,000.00**
- Same hours billed hourly: **₹60,000.00**
- Discount given: **₹9,000.00** a month
- Agreement value: **₹6,12,000.00**
- Effective rate: **₹1,700.00** an hour

**What this means for Kiran:** the discount costs ₹1,08,000 over the year. That's the price of a full year's guaranteed income, so it's worth agreeing knowingly.

### 3. A UK designer whose client uses half the hours

Sophie charges £75 an hour for a 10-hour monthly retainer over 3 months, with no discount. The client only uses half the hours.

- Monthly retainer fee: **£750.00**
- Same hours billed hourly: **£750.00**
- Discount given: **£0.00**
- Agreement value: **£2,250.00**
- Effective rate: **£150.00** an hour

**What this means for Sophie:** unused hours double her effective rate. If the client keeps using half, they may ask to reduce the hours, so it's worth discussing at the first review.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Fix your hourly rate first.** A retainer at the wrong rate stays wrong for months.
- **Keep the discount small**, or offer priority response instead of money off.
- **State the unused-hours rule** before the client signs.
- **Invoice at the start of the month.** The fee pays for reserved time, not finished work.
- **Review every 3 to 6 months.** Adjust the hours to real use, and raise your rate when the time comes with the <ToolLink slug="rate-increase-calculator">rate increase calculator</ToolLink>.
