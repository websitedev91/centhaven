## What this calculator does

This calculator works out the **minimum hourly rate** you need to charge as a freelancer to reach the take-home income you want.

It adds your tax and business expenses on top of that income. Then it spreads the total over the hours you can actually bill, not every hour you work.

## How it works

You need enough revenue to cover three things: your take-home pay, the tax on it, and your business costs. You can only earn that money in billable hours.

First, it grosses up your take-home income for tax and adds your expenses. Next, it counts your billable hours: working weeks × hours per week × the share of time you can bill. Finally, it divides one by the other.

```text
grossNeeded    = targetIncome ÷ (1 − taxRate) + expenses
billableHours  = (52 − weeksOff) × hoursPerWeek × billable%
hourlyRate     = grossNeeded ÷ billableHours
dayRate        = hourlyRate × (hoursPerWeek ÷ 5)
monthlyRevenue = grossNeeded ÷ 12
```

## Worked examples

### 1. A US web designer

Maya wants to take home $60,000 a year. Her software, laptop and insurance cost $6,000 a year. She sets aside 25% for tax, takes 6 weeks off and works 40 hours a week. About 75% of that time is billable.

- Gross revenue needed: $60,000 ÷ 0.75 + $6,000 = **$86,000.00** a year (**$7,166.67** a month)
- Billable hours: 46 weeks × 40 × 75% = **1,380** hours
- Minimum hourly rate: $86,000 ÷ 1,380 = **$62.32**
- Day rate (8 hours): **$498.55**

### 2. An Indian software developer

Arjun wants ₹12,00,000 a year after tax. His expenses are ₹1,20,000 a year and he estimates a 10% tax rate. He takes 4 weeks off and works 40 hours a week, but only 60% of that is billable because of client calls and sales work.

- Gross revenue needed: **₹14,53,333.33** a year (**₹1,21,111.11** a month)
- Billable hours: 48 × 40 × 60% = **1,152** hours
- Minimum hourly rate: **₹1,261.57**
- Day rate (8 hours): **₹10,092.59**

### 3. A part-time freelancer in the UK

Sam freelances 30 hours a week and wants £50,000 a year. Sam's partner covers the business costs, so expenses are £0. To keep it simple, Sam leaves tax at 0%, takes 2 weeks off and bills every hour worked.

- Gross revenue needed: **£50,000.00** a year (**£4,166.67** a month)
- Billable hours: 50 × 30 × 100% = **1,500** hours
- Minimum hourly rate: **£33.33**
- Day rate (6 hours): **£200.00**

Sam's real rate should be higher, because nobody bills 100% of their time and tax is rarely zero. Change those two inputs above and watch the rate climb.

## Tips and common mistakes

- **Don't forget unpaid time.** Admin, invoicing, sales calls and learning all take hours you can't bill. Most freelancers bill 60–80% of their working time.
- **Add a buffer for slow months.** Work rarely arrives evenly. Many freelancers add 10–20% on top of the minimum rate so a quiet month doesn't hurt.
- **Count all your expenses.** Software, equipment, internet, insurance, accounting fees, platform fees and training all belong in the expenses box.
- **Use a realistic tax rate.** Include income tax and any self-employment or social security contributions. If you're not sure, ask an accountant.
- **Review your rate every year.** Your costs rise and your skills grow. Check your numbers at least once a year.
- **This is your floor, not your price.** The result is the least you can charge to hit your goal. Your market value may be much higher.
