This back pay calculator works out the gross amount owed when a raise started late or pay periods were missed. Choose an hourly raise, a salary raise or missed pay, enter your own numbers, and see the total before tax. It only does the maths: every rate, salary, hour and week comes from you.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose what happened.** Hourly raise paid late, Salary raise paid late, or Missed pay.
2. **Choose your currency.**
3. **Enter your numbers.** For an hourly raise: the old and new hourly rates, your hours per week and the weeks owed. For a salary raise: the old and new yearly salaries and the weeks owed. For missed pay: the gross pay for one period and how many periods were missed.

The starting values are examples only. Use the figures from your payslips or contract.

## How to calculate back pay

```text
hourly raise:  back pay = (new rate − old rate) × hours per week × weeks owed
salary raise:  back pay = (new salary − old salary) ÷ 52 × weeks owed
missed pay:    back pay = pay per period × periods missed
```

## Back pay for a pay raise: worked examples

### 1. An hourly raise

Your rate went from $20 to $22 an hour, you work 40 hours a week, and the raise was 12 weeks late.

- Difference per hour: **$2.00**
- Difference per week: 2 × 40 = **$80.00**
- Back pay: 80 × 12 = **$960.00**

**What this means:** before tax, the late raise adds up to $960 for those 12 weeks.

### 2. A salary raise

Your salary went from $50,000 to $55,000, and the new salary started 10 weeks late.

- Difference per year: **$5,000.00**
- Difference per week: 5,000 ÷ 52 = **$96.15**
- Back pay: **$961.54**

### 3. In rupees

Your hourly rate went from ₹300 to ₹330 for 40 hours a week, 12 weeks late.

- Difference per week: **₹1,200.00**
- Back pay: **₹14,400.00**

## Missed pay calculator: whole periods

If whole paychecks were missed, multiply one paycheck by the number missed. At $1,500 every two weeks, three missed paychecks come to **$4,500.00**. Part of a period works too: 2.5 periods of $2,250 is **$5,625.00**. The pay period setting only changes the words in the result.

## Retro pay calculator: getting the inputs right

Use gross pay, before tax, for every figure, and count the weeks from when the new pay should have started to when it actually did. If your hours or rate changed along the way, work out each stretch separately and add the totals together. Overtime, shift pay or bonuses are not included unless you add them to the figures you enter.
