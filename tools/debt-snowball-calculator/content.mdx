This debt snowball calculator pays off up to five debts month by month in two ways and shows them side by side. The snowball puts extra money on the smallest balance first. The avalanche puts it on the highest interest rate first. For each one you see how long it takes, the total interest, the total you pay and the order your debts are cleared.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose your currency.** The starting values are examples only.
2. **Enter each debt:** its balance, its interest rate (APR) from your statement and its minimum payment. Add up to five debts, or remove the ones you don't need.
3. **Add an extra payment** each month, on top of the minimums, if you can. Leave it at 0 to pay minimums only.

The result shows how soon you can be debt-free, both methods side by side, the difference in interest and a chart of the total debt left each month.

## How the debt payoff calculator works

```text
monthly rate    = APR ÷ 100 ÷ 12
monthly payment = all minimums + extra   (the same every month)

each month:
  1. add interest to every debt: balance × monthly rate
  2. pay every debt its minimum (or what is left of it)
  3. put the rest on the target debt; when it is cleared,
     the rest goes to the next target

snowball target  = smallest starting balance first
avalanche target = highest interest rate first
```

When a debt is cleared, its minimum stays in your monthly payment and rolls over to the next target. Lenders may work out interest a little differently (daily interest, fees), so treat the result as an estimate.

## Snowball vs avalanche calculator: worked examples

### 1. Three debts with an extra payment

Someone owes $2,500 at 12% (minimum $75), $6,000 at 24% (minimum $150) and $9,000 at 7% (minimum $250). They pay $200 extra, so $675 a month in total.

- Snowball: **31 months**, total interest **$3,282.11**. Order: Debt 1 (month 10) → Debt 2 (month 26) → Debt 3 (month 31)
- Avalanche: **31 months**, total interest **$2,878.68**. Order: Debt 2 (month 22) → Debt 1 (month 25) → Debt 3 (month 31)
- The avalanche saves **$403.43** in interest

**What this means:** both are debt-free in the same month. The avalanche costs less, while the snowball clears its first debt 12 months sooner.

### 2. Two debts

Someone owes $3,000 at 20% (minimum $90) and $1,500 at 10% (minimum $50), with $100 extra.

- Snowball: **23 months**, interest **$841.34**
- Avalanche: **22 months**, interest **$702.05**
- The avalanche saves **$139.29** and 1 month

### 3. When both methods agree

Someone owes ₹50,000 at 36%, ₹2,00,000 at 14% and ₹1,20,000 at 18%, with ₹5,000 extra a month. The smallest debt also has the highest rate, so both methods pay the same debt first. Both take **26 months** and cost **₹68,391.07** in interest.

## Debt avalanche calculator: choosing a method

The avalanche saves the same or more interest whenever the monthly payment is the same. The snowball clears whole debts sooner, which many people find keeps them going. The calculator shows both with your own numbers, so you can see what the difference costs and pick the one you will stick with.

## How long to pay off debt

The biggest lever is the extra payment. In the first example, paying minimums only takes 51 months and $6,381.50 in interest. The $200 extra cuts that to 31 months. If your payment doesn't cover the interest, the calculator tells you instead of showing a date.
