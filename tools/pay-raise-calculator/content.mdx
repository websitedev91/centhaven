This pay raise calculator works out your new pay after a raise, or the raise percentage between your old and new pay. It works for pay per hour, week, month or year, in any of six currencies, and shows what the change is worth over a whole year.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Choose what you know.** Pick **The raise %** if you know the percentage, or **My new pay** if you know both amounts.
2. **Choose your currency and pay period.** The starting values are examples only, so replace them with your own pay.
3. **Enter your current pay** before tax, then the raise % or your new pay.
4. **For hourly pay, enter your hours per week** so the calculator can show yearly figures.
5. **Add an inflation figure** if you want to see your raise after rising prices. Leave it at 0 to skip it.

The result shows your new pay or your raise percentage, the change per period and per year.

## How the salary increase calculator works

```text
new pay       = current pay × (1 + raise %)
raise %       = (new pay − current pay) ÷ current pay
change a year = change per period × periods in a year
real raise    = (1 + raise %) ÷ (1 + inflation %) − 1
```

A year has 52 weeks and 12 months. For hourly pay, a year is your hours per week × 52.

## Raise percentage calculator: worked examples

### 1. A 5% raise on a salary

Emma earns $60,000 a year and gets a 5% raise.

- New salary: **$63,000.00**
- Raise: **$3,000.00** a year

**What this means for Emma:** the raise is worth $250.00 a month before tax.

### 2. Finding the percentage

Daniel's salary goes from $50,000 to $54,000.

- Raise: **8%**
- Change: **$4,000.00** a year

**What this means for Daniel:** he can compare his 8% with the raises he has had before, or with an offer from another employer.

### 3. A raise on an Indian salary

Priya earns ₹12,00,000 a year and gets a 10% raise.

- New salary: **₹13,20,000.00**
- Raise: **₹1,20,000.00** a year

**What this means for Priya:** that's ₹10,000.00 more each month before tax.

### 4. An hourly wage

Tom earns $25 an hour, works 40 hours a week and gets a 4% raise.

- New wage: **$26.00** an hour
- Change per year: **$2,080.00**
- New pay per year: **$54,080.00**

**What this means for Tom:** a raise of $1 an hour adds up to more than two thousand dollars over a year.

## How much is a 5% raise after inflation?

A raise only makes you better off if it grows faster than prices. If your pay rises by 5% and prices rise by 3%, your real raise is about **1.94%**, not 2%, because the inflation applies to the new, higher amount. Enter an inflation figure you trust in the optional box to see your own real raise. We don't suggest a figure, because it depends on where you live and what you buy.

## New salary after raise: tips

- **Compare per year.** A small hourly or weekly raise looks bigger once you see it over a full year.
- **Look at take-home pay too.** The calculator works before tax. Your payslip will show what you actually receive.
- **Know both numbers before you ask.** Have the percentage and the amount ready for your conversation.
