This margin vs markup calculator shows the profit on a sale both ways at once: as a share of the price (margin) and as a share of the cost (markup). Enter a cost and a selling price to see both, enter a cost and a target margin or markup to find a price, or convert one percentage into the other.

## How to use the calculator

1. **Pick a mode.** Cost and price shows margin and markup for a price you already charge. Find a price works out the price you need. Convert % turns a margin into a markup, or the other way round.
2. **Choose your currency** and enter your **cost**: what one item or job costs you. The starting values are examples only.
3. **Enter a selling price**, or choose whether to price from a **margin** or a **markup** and enter the percentage.

The result shows margin, markup and the profit on each sale. A bar under the calculator shows how the price splits into cost and profit.

## Margin vs markup: the formulas

```text
profit = price − cost
margin = profit ÷ price
markup = profit ÷ cost

price from a margin = cost ÷ (1 − margin)
price from a markup = cost × (1 + markup)

markup from a margin = margin ÷ (1 − margin)
margin from a markup = markup ÷ (1 + markup)
```

Work with decimals in the formulas: 40% is 0.40.

## Worked examples

### 1. One product, two percentages

A shop buys an item for $60 and sells it for $100.

- Profit per sale: **$40.00**
- Margin: **40%** (40 ÷ 100)
- Markup: **66.67%** (40 ÷ 60)

**What this means:** the same $40 looks bigger as a markup, because the cost is a smaller number than the price.

### 2. Pricing from a markup

A maker's product costs $50, and they add a 50% markup.

- Selling price: **$75.00**
- Profit per sale: **$25.00**
- Margin: **33.33%**

### 3. Selling price from margin, in rupees

A seller's item costs ₹800, and they want a 20% margin.

- Selling price: **₹1,000.00** (800 ÷ 0.80)
- Profit per sale: **₹200.00**
- Markup: **25%**

**What this means:** adding 20% to the cost would give only ₹960. To keep 20% of the price as profit, the price has to be ₹1,000.

### 4. Same 50%, different meaning

A 50% markup on a $50 cost gives a $75 price, which is a 33.33% margin. A 50% margin on the same cost needs a $100 price, which is a 100% markup. Saying "we make 50%" can mean either, so it pays to say which one you mean.

## Markup to margin calculator: quick conversions

Switch to Convert % to change one into the other without any money:

- A 25% margin = a **33.33%** markup
- A 25% markup = a **20%** margin
- A 50% margin = a **100%** markup

Margin is always below 100%. Markup can be any size: a 200% markup means the price is three times the cost.

## Margin to markup calculator: when it matters

Most accounts and reports show margin, because they look at profit as a share of sales. Many people set prices with markup, because they start from what something costs. When a supplier, a sales team and an accountant use different ones, a target of 40% can mean very different prices. Converting first keeps everyone on the same number.

If you sell below cost, both numbers turn negative. A $120 cost and a $100 price is a −20% margin and a −16.67% markup, and the calculator warns you.
